Shop documentation & launch playbooks

Why it was needed

With shops in different cities and new people starting all the time, too much knowledge lived in people's heads, in WhatsApp chats and in folders only a few people could find their way around. The same questions kept coming back, and every new opening started from scratch.

What I did

Nobody asked me to; I started because I kept answering the same things. I looked at who needed what: a barista on their first shift, a shop manager with a problem, an intern in their first week, the team opening a new shop. Then I wrote for each of them.

  • For shifts: SOPs, opening and closing checklists, a cleaning plan and an information sheet for each shop.
  • For openings: a launch plan running from six weeks before opening to 30 days after, an opening-day runbook and a set-up list for new shops.
  • For new people: training material, the Welcome Book, step-by-step hiring guides with reusable emails, and a handbook for interns explaining how the company and the shop network work.
  • To find it all: a clear structure for the operations Drive, with an overview of where everything lives.

What came out of it

Shop standards and launch steps written down in one place instead of passed on by word of mouth. The launch documents were used for the opening of the Waasland kiosk. The documents themselves are internal, so only their types are shown here.

IzyCoffee Corner

Why it was needed

IzyCoffee wanted to grow beyond its own shops, but a classic franchise felt too heavy and bureaucratic for the business.

What I did

I researched how other coffee chains expand and compared franchise and licensing models. Based on that, I suggested a supply-licensing approach: partners run the location, while IzyCoffee keeps control of the brand, product standards, training and supply. The final decisions were the company's, not mine alone.

Once the direction was set, I prepared what partners would need:

  • Partner pitch deck
  • Frequently asked questions
  • Welcome Book
  • Partner training guide
  • Trainee manual (4 sessions, 16 hours)
  • How to train your team
  • Sales & upselling playbook

What came out of it

A complete partner toolkit, from the first pitch to training. The first IzyCoffee Corner runs inside a partner business in Merchtem.

Kitchr pilot

Why it was needed

Operational information was spread across WhatsApp, Excel, Google Drive, email and PDFs. Training wasn't consistent, and managers spent a lot of time chasing information.

What I did

The platform was built by external developers; I'm not a developer. My part was explaining how the shops actually work and making sure the platform followed that. I mapped the workflows, turned what the shops needed into requirements, helped decide which features came first, tested the pilot and fed back what didn't match daily shop work.

What came out of it

A pilot built around real shop routines, with SOPs, daily checklists and location information in one place.
